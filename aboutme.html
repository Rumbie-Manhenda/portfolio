A list of technologies tells you what I use. It does not tell you much about me. So here is the longer version - where I come from, what set me alight, and what I am actually like to work with.

Get to know me

Curiosity has taken me further from home than I ever planned.

I grew up in Zimbabwe, studied in the UK, spent several years in Finland, and now live and work in Athens, Greece. Along the way I collected degrees, jobs, experiences, mistakes, lessons - and probably far too many questions.

What I enjoy has never really been the certificate at the end of it. It is the moment a messy, unfamiliar problem finally gives way, and I understand why it was breaking.

  • Why does this work?
  • Why doesn't that work?
  • What happens if I change this?
  • And, most often - can I work it out myself?

That habit of asking is what eventually led me to programming, and then to artificial intelligence and machine learning. I had found my kind of playground.

The work itself

For me, building something is only half the adventure. I want to understand what happens beneath the surface: the logic, the assumptions, and why things work or fail. I pull things apart, experiment, ask questions, and put the pieces back together with a little more understanding than before.

I don't always find the answers quickly. But the process of getting closer to them is what makes the work rewarding.

Journey so far

I started out sceptical about technology. Then I began to wonder what it could become in the right hands.

I was uneasy about what technology might do to people, to society, and to our sense of right and wrong. Omar Bradley's warning challenged the way I was looking at it. Maybe the real question was not whether technology is good or bad, but who is building it, why, and whether they have the judgement to use it well.

That changed the direction of my life. Driven by curiosity, I chose Computer Science and discovered that I genuinely enjoyed solving problems and understanding how things work. Along the way I explored front-end development and UX/UI design, and learned that technology is not only about making things work - it is also about making them useful and understandable.

Then I encountered artificial intelligence, and something clicked. I found myself less interested in simply building software and more fascinated by the thinking behind intelligent systems: how machines learn, how we teach them, and what happens when they start making decisions that affect people.

The road has not been straight. There were difficult seasons and a long stretch where I struggled with my mental health, and volunteering and poetry became unexpected sources of strength. Scholarships - including the STEM Scholarship Programme and the Varadi Scholarship - helped make higher education possible, and I went on to earn my Bachelor's degree in Computer Science with First-Class Honours.

Today I am still learning, still asking questions, and still trying to understand where technology meets people, society and responsibility. From Computer Science to artificial intelligence, and now towards Science, Technology and Society, the questions have grown bigger - and so has my curiosity.
